# Task Records

This document defines the research-project requirements for durable records of PostCode application development. The application repository owns the operative protocol that satisfies these requirements.

## Purpose

Implementing PostCode may expose considerations that affect the product design, research strategy, methodology, roadmap, or other research-project documents independently of observations produced by using PostCode. Durable task records allow the research process to examine those implementation outcomes without treating the application repository's development workflow as part of the research methodology.

Task records are application-development artifacts. They are not, merely by existing, research observations from PostCode use.

## Required Record

Each substantive application-development task must leave a durable record that preserves:

- the approved task instructions or goal, retaining the original wording when practical;
- material follow-ups that clarify, constrain, redirect, pause, or extend the authorized work;
- the task's final disposition;
- the implemented outcome and any material deviation from the approved task;
- unresolved concerns and resulting follow-up work or backlog entries; and
- the verification performed and its result.

The records and repository history together must make the relationship among the approved work, material changes in direction, implementation changes, verification, and conclusion inspectable. The application protocol may choose the exact mechanism.

## Historical Integrity

Task records must be version-controlled and remain available after conclusion. Their chronology must not be silently rewritten to reflect later understanding. Corrections, qualifications, and continuation work should preserve the earlier account and make the later change visible.

The application protocol determines which work is substantive enough to require a record. It need not turn every exploratory conversation, documentation adjustment, or incidental cleanup into a task.

## Application-Owned Protocol

The application repository must maintain an operative task protocol that satisfies these requirements and is available to its development agents. It may define and evolve its own paths, templates, identifiers, status vocabulary, commit conventions, worktree handling, and other lifecycle mechanics.

Changes to that protocol do not require a research-foundation revision while the required record remains available and interpretable. When a protocol change materially affects what the records capture or how task outcomes can be compared, the research process should take that change into account.

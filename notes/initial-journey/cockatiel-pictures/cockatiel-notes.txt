CAPTIONS AND CLAIM-TO-SOURCE MAP

01 / THE APPLICATION OWNS THE JOB; POLICIES GOVERN EXECUTION
Caption: Separate the application's business lifecycle from policy-controlled
execution, and distinguish reusable policy state from progress in one call.

Source heading: "What Cockatiel does and who owns what".
- First paragraph: application supplies an operation; policy applies configured
  rules; execution yields an eventual value or error; business meaning is not
  required by Cockatiel.
- Second paragraph: application creates and retains instances and chooses
  sharing; owns identities, preparation, acknowledgments and stored outcomes.
  Breaker state and bulkhead counts/queue persist across calls; retry attempts
  are per execution. There is no automatic grouping by contacted service.
- Policy table: the retry-attempt illustration draws on retry's broad role.

02 / STARTING, WAITING AND SUCCEEDING ARE DIFFERENT
Caption: Compare starting, acceptance into an in-memory wait, and refusal;
then see why a capacity/state snapshot does not promise future admission.

Source heading: "Admission, waiting and recovery".
- First two paragraphs: immediate start, accepted waiting and success differ;
  inspection reserves no permission; bulkhead exposes execution and queue
  capacity; queued work remains pending in memory, without a durable job lifecycle.
- Third paragraph: closed/open/half-open roles, manual isolation, and the
  default single-trial example. Other calls wait for the trial and face the
  resulting decision, which can refuse them. Half-open is not proof of health.
Source heading: "What failures and notifications tell the caller".
- First paragraph: admission refusals have distinct error types; waiting
  itself is not failure. The picture does not name or collapse those types.

03 / THE OUTSIDE POLICY CONTROLS CALLS INTO THE INSIDE
Caption: Nested boundaries show what is already held or admitted when a
call encounters a second policy. The retry loop shows repeated entry through
the same breaker, rather than a bypass around it.

Source heading: "How policies work together".
- First paragraph: wrapping retains component instances and separate states;
  outer policies control calls into the next policy.
- Second paragraph: both order-dependent waiting cases and retry outside
  a breaker are explicitly described. These are possible situations, not
  unconditional outcomes of those compositions.
Source heading: "What failures and notifications tell the caller".
- First two paragraphs: outer retry/fallback may handle an inner failure;
  events describe local activity, not one composition-wide report.

04 / MOVE THE TRANSITION, NOT THE WORK
Caption: Align current and proposed behavior at the same three landmarks.
The proposed timer changes when state becomes half-open; application work
still needs a later execution, and successful recovery is still unknown.

Source heading: "Admission, waiting and recovery".
- Fourth and fifth paragraphs: opening records time/delay; a later eligible
  execution triggers half-open and supplies the trial. Time passage and state
  reads alone do not transition. Existing events report actual transitions;
  delay expiry alone currently emits no transition event.
Source heading: "Human intent and the chosen direction".
- "Reported need": costly preparation and state-check suppression of recovery;
  this is reported, not reproduced. The reporter's timing workaround is not drawn.
- "Human preference": immediate scope, existing facilities, room to grow.
- "Chosen direction within the simulation": opt-in automatic transition at
  recovery-delay expiry, default unchanged, existing state/event facility,
  no automatic work and no established successful recovery.
- Endorsement and "Still to settle": no implementation/handoff authorization,
  no maintainer acceptance; all listed unresolved categories appear in the view.
Source heading: "How the account and the design direction changed".
- Final two table rows distinguish the factual correction about lazy
  transitions from the design choice to prefer automatic opt-in transitions
  over the earlier generic acceptance-query proposal.

VISUAL LANGUAGE AND ASSUMPTIONS

Tickets denote operation invocations, except the explicitly labelled business
job folder in view 1. They are not messages, threads, durable queue entries,
or a claim about data representation. A repeated ticket along an arrow is the
same call at successive positions. Arrows show call progression, return,
repetition or time as labelled; they do not specify physical transport.

Open-topped slots show occupancy; nested outlines show policy wrapping, not
deployment or ownership of the supplied work. The circuit symbol deliberately
uses electrical semantics: a closed circuit allows passage, an open circuit
withholds it. The half-open arrow means limited trials, not a fractional opening.
Solid account illustrations, the dashed purple proposal, a labelled human
preference, and question-mark items distinguish epistemic/design status without
depending on colour alone. Red means blocking/refusal here, not policy failure
classification in general. Amber marks waiting/trial situations.

Illustrative choices, not configured defaults or measured traces:
- View 1 uses three retry-progress marks solely to show per-call progress.
- View 2 chooses one execution slot and one queue slot for visible occupancy.
  Its snapshot example assumes another call takes capacity between inspection
  and execution. No ordering or fairness rule is implied.
- View 3A assumes a trial is already active and the outer bulkhead has admitted
  the pictured waiting call. The "busy" indicator is breaker status, not a
  second call occupying that same slot. The active trial's origin is not shown.
- View 3B assumes capacity is held by other work and queue space is available;
  that other work's origin is unspecified. Repeated trial tickets show one call.
- View 4 shows ordinary recovery with no intervening isolation, reopening or
  restoration. Those cases are unresolved, not covered by the timeline. Its
  notice/inspect-to-prepare arrow is a possible application use of the proposed
  facility, not a mandated workflow, exact event sequence, or reservation.

GAPS AND CONSEQUENTIAL LIMITATIONS

The account leaves timer ordering, stale/duplicate transitions, restoration,
lifetime/liveness, compatibility and reproduction unresolved. There is no
basis here for an exact implementation, timer API, schedule or verified test.
Notification is shown as a facility, not a promise about event order or delivery.

These views select the account's operating and recovery story. They omit the
full policy family; failure-selection configuration versus executable policy
naming; unsuccessful returned results versus exceptions; the breaker-refusal
exception to ordinary operation-failure events; and direct breaker-reference
availability as an application-interface choice. The generic acceptance-query
contract was not completed. Inspection is not presented as that missing API.

They do not cover other recovery-sampling configurations, cancellation details,
or an overall execution budget across attempts, delays, queues and recovery
waiting. The account explicitly leaves the budget investigation unstarted.
The pictures are not a complete state machine and do not establish why the
maintainers chose lazy transitions. The account labels that rationale inference.
